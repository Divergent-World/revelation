MOVEMENT I

The Scroll
Opens

Revelation 1 — 7  ·  Fifteen registers

History is not random. It is sealed, owned, and opened only by one found worthy. The first movement begins on Patmos with a man receiving a vision he did not ask for, and rises — within a dozen registers — into the throne room where the fate of the world is disclosed. Awe turns into dread. By the closing plate the Horsemen are already riding.

Three of these fifteen registers no longer exist. The reader panel that opens the whole cycle is among them: the figure of John that every subsequent movement answers to was lost, and has been rebuilt here from the five that survive.

Note on the plate

The Angers weaver gave Christ a mandorla and left the sword floating free of the mouth — a solution to an image the text makes almost unpaintable. I have kept the floating sword. It is the one place in this movement where I let the medieval reading overrule my own.

THE REGISTER OF LOSSES

Concordance of the ninety slots · survival status after the 1782 dispersal
Of the ninety registers woven at Angers between 1377 and 1382, seventy-one survive in some condition. The rest were cut up for horse blankets, used to lag orangery pipes, or simply vanished. This book reconstructs all ninety. The status column records what was actually there to work from — and therefore how much of each plate is evidence and how much is argument.

COLOPHON

This book was set in EB Garamond, cut after the sixteenth-century romans of Claude Garamont; in Cinzel, after Roman inscriptional capitals; and in Space Grotesk, which carries the apparatus — the slot numbers, the survival codes, the plate data. The two families are not meant to reconcile. One belongs to the manuscript, the other to the system that rebuilt it.

The plates were generated between March and August of 2026 and selected from roughly one hundred and sixty candidates. Each was made against a fixed style contract and a coordinate system for viewer space, so that ninety images made across six months would read as one hand.
